Understanding 💡

What a discussion text is, why we write it, and how it differs from an exposition.

A fair judge listens to both sides! 👂
📖 Definition

What is a discussion text?

“A discussion text presents an issue from at least two points of view (arguments for and against) before giving a conclusion or recommendation.

IDTeks diskusi menyajikan suatu isu dari dua sudut pandang atau lebih (argumen pro dan kontra), lalu ditutup dengan kesimpulan atau rekomendasi.

🎯 Social function

Why do people discuss issues?

❓

To present an issue

Introduce a controversial topic that people disagree about.

⚖️

To show both sides

Give arguments for and against in a fair, balanced way.

🏁

To help decide

End with a wise conclusion or recommendation after weighing everything.

Discussion vs Exposition

⚖️ DISCUSSION

Sides
Presents at least two points of view
Structure
Issue → For → Against → Conclusion
Writer
Stays balanced; may decide only at the end
Typical language
Some people argue… · On the other hand…
VS

📣 EXPOSITION

Sides
Presents one point of view only
Structure
Thesis → Arguments → Recommendation / Reiteration
Writer
Takes a side from the first paragraph
Typical language
I strongly believe… · Firstly… · Therefore…

🔑 Quick test: Does the text give the other side a fair chance? Yes → discussion · No, only one side → exposition

🧱 Level up · XI–XII

Two patterns, two endings

🧱 Block pattern

All the arguments for first, then all the arguments against. Easy to follow.

IssueFor 1 · For 2 · For 3Against 1 · Against 2 · Against 3Conclusion

🔀 Point-by-point

One aspect at a time, with its for and against side by side. Great for comparing.

IssueCost: for ⇄ againstHealth: for ⇄ againstConclusion
🤝 Neutral conclusion
Sums up both sides without choosing: “All in all, the answer depends on how … is used.”
📣 Conclusion + recommendation
Weighs both sides, then suggests an action: “Having considered both sides, schools should …”

📦 Segmentation

What goes inside each part?

① Issuethe question
Short background + the controversial question; shows that people disagree
1 paragraph“There is a debate about whether…” · “Should…?”
② Arguments Forthe supporting side
2–3 points that support the issue, each with a reason, fact or example
1–2 paragraphs“Supporters argue that… Moreover…”
③ Arguments Againstthe opposing side
2–3 points that oppose the issue, with the same amount of detail
1–2 paragraphs“On the other hand, critics believe…”
④ Conclusion+ recommendation
Sums up both sides, then gives a balanced view or a recommendation
1 paragraph“Having considered both sides, …”
04 ROUND 04

Language Features 🔤

The words that keep your text fair, careful and convincing.

“However” is my favourite word! 😄
⚡ Fair-play tools

Six language features

🕒

Simple present tense

For facts, general truths and current opinions.

Phones distract students.
👥

Generic participants

Groups of people, not “I”, to stay objective.

supporters · critics · some people · experts
🗣️

Reporting & thinking verbs

Show whose opinion it is.

argue · claim · believe · point out
↔️

Contrastive connectives

Switch from one side to the other.

However · On the other hand · Although · While
➕

Additive & causal connectives

Add points and explain results.

Moreover · In addition · As a result · because
🎚️

Modals & hedging

Sound careful, not extreme; recommend politely.

may · might · could · perhaps · should
🖍️ Highlighter👆 nyalakan warna, bisa lebih dari satu

Spot the features

IssuePhones at School · Today, almost every teenager owns a smartphone. Some schools allow students to bring phones, while others ban them completely. Should students be allowed to bring phones to school?
ForSupporters argue that phones help students learn. With a phone, students can search for information, use online dictionaries and join learning apps. Moreover, phones keep students safe, because parents can contact them quickly in an emergency.
AgainstOn the other hand, many teachers believe that phones distract students. Notifications from games and social media can break their concentration. Furthermore, phones may cause problems such as cheating and cyberbullying.
ConclusionIn conclusion, phones bring both benefits and risks. Perhaps the best solution is to allow phones at school but to set clear rules for using them.
